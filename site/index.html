The difference is where the decisions get made: before the code, or after it breaks.

Prompt, code, hope

Example

"Add a booking page to my salon site."

  1. Claude writes the page, the form and the database straight away.
  2. Two clients book the same slot. You ask it to fix that.
  3. The fix breaks the confirmation email. You ask again.
  4. Nobody decided what happens on a cancellation, so it guesses.

You're debugging decisions nobody made.

With Frontier

Example

"Add a booking page to my salon site."

  1. Asks first: what happens when two people pick the same slot? On a cancellation? Who gets the email?
  2. Writes it down as a one-page plan you can read and change.
  3. Splits it into small tickets: slots, booking, emails.
  4. Builds the first ticket, test first, on its own branch, then stops and waits for your go.

Every decision made before a line of code.

Nothing changes until you say go.

Steps that only look run straight away: questions, critiques, code reviews.

Steps that change files tell you what will change, then wait. Each ticket is built on its own branch, so your working version stays untouched until you keep the change.

Six steps, handed to you one at a time.

You don't memorise these. Say start here and Frontier hands over each one. Tap a command to copy it.

  1. 01

    Question

    Hard questions until every decision about your idea is made. Nothing is written yet.

  2. 02

    Plan

    The agreed plan, written down where you already keep work: GitHub, GitLab, Jira, Linear or plain files.

  3. 03

    Split

    Small tickets, each one buildable and checkable on its own.

  4. 04

    Build

    One ticket at a time, test first, on its own branch. Your tests are re-run before it's ticked off.

  5. 05

    Polish

    Screens critiqued and fixed. Words stripped of anything that sounds machine-written.

  6. 06

    Review

    The change checked against the plan, then handed to you to keep or send back.
